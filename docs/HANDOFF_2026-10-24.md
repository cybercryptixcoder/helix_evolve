## 1. What Helix is

An LLM-driven evolutionary code search, built by Shreyas Naik (with Sai Vikram) for a hackathon on October 11, 2026,
and developed since.
- **The search:** programs are evolved. Each program writes a construction (a solution); an evaluator scores it.
  Every program is kept in a tree.
- **The model:** DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, through Luminal. The keys are in `.env` (never commit it, or any key).
- **The two problems:**
  - **ac2,** the second autocorrelation inequality: maximise R(f) = ‖f∗f‖₂² / (‖f∗f‖₁ ‖f∗f‖∞) for a non-negative step
    function f (`helix4/problems/ac2.py`).
  - **A private VRP,** through a plug-in that lives outside this repo (`docs/VRP_CONTEXT.md`).

**The v4 false ceiling** (`docs/V4_CEILING.md`) had these causes:
- 72% of children returned their start unchanged;
- experiments were judged against the champion within 30 s;
- the map was all failures;
- 1e-9 gains counted as records;
- code was clipped at 12k characters;
- the planner's analyses called the construction "saturated" in 15% of cases at first and 95% later.

**v7's results** (`docs/V7_RESULTS.md`):
- Backtracks were short steps back.
- Restarts from scratch all fell into the model's default basin: 19 of 19 at or below the textbook 0.8825 on ac2.
- Revive gave 0 gains, because revive tracks were judged against unchanged copies of the record.
- 68% of ac2 children were unchanged.
- The planner questioned shared assumptions 9 times on the VRP and 3 on ac2. The ac2 bold slot's wording, "build from
  nothing", sent every bold experiment to scratch.

**v8** (`helix8/`):
- **Hills:** a hill is a family of programs whose outputs are nearly the same. On ac2: the construction's shape (64
  bins, total-variation distance, mirror-invariant; radius 0.075). On the VRP: the trip sets of the solutions
  (radius 0.12).
- **The register** (`hills.jsonl`). Each track's best lands on the nearest hill, or founds a new hill after a
  follow-up. A track carrying a hill moves its top. Code Zero's hill is fixed.
- **Moved-only track bests:** unchanged copies of the start don't count.
- **The slots:** flip (change a decision every hill shares, keeping the chain) replaces revive. Restart (unlike every
  hill, applying the chain at once; stops on landing on a known hill) replaces bold. Both get 2 follow-ups.
- **The Cartographer:** describes hills and lists shared decisions.
- **The planner** sees the map.

**Calibration:**
- ac2: the textbook bests of 6 runs are within 0.04 of each other; polishing over 100 generations moved 0.06; different
  families are 0.09–0.6 apart.
- VRP: noise 0.02; climbing steps 0.03–0.08; the change of hill 0.20.

**Standard to keep in any next version:**

- v4.7's measurement fixes;
- v7's levels, tracks, nothing closed and the main slot;
- v8's moved-only bests and code-measured hills.

## 5. What has been learnt

- **Exploration choices matter more than how much exploration there is.** v7 explored in 93 of 100 ac2 generations and
  found little.
- **The model's restarts aren't random restarts.** They sample its prior and collapse into one basin.
- **New hills come from changing a decision every program shares**, then climbing past the initial drop.
- **Seeds only drive parent sampling.** The model's randomness dominates the run-to-run spread; small comparisons
  between versions mostly measure noise. Prefer event-level readings: which mechanism produced which gain.
- **The planner's context saturates, not the model.** The Atlas fills with failures. A fresh Atlas did better than the
  old one in the v4.7 warm runs. This is the hypothesis behind the "restart effect" (task 11).
- **On the VRP, program bloat is a risk:** 55k → 132k characters in 100 generations, near what one reply can hold.
